# END_SCREEN

Pixel art, VGA 256-colour palette, 640x480 pixels (displayed at 320x240), style of early 1990s point-and-click adventure games such as Police Quest or King's Quest VI. Hard pixel edges throughout, no anti-aliasing, no modern rendering techniques. Muted period-appropriate colour palette. This is an ending/outro screen, not a gameplay location.

The viewpoint stands on the stone steps of the house's front door, looking outward and away from the house entirely - no part of the manor's facade appears anywhere in the frame. Stone lions flank the steps in the immediate foreground, rendered as pale weathered silhouettes. From here a long gravel drive, dusted with snow, stretches away down the centre of the composition in exaggerated perspective, the distance to the wrought-iron park gates considerably greater than the width of the drive itself - the gates appear small and distant, well back toward the vanishing point rather than filling any significant portion of the frame. The focal point, well down the drive and close to the open gates, is an old black 1920s police saloon, viewed square-on from directly behind as it departs, receding straight away from the viewer toward the gap between the gates, having just carried the detective away from the house - rendered large enough to read clearly despite the distance, showing a rounded roofline, folded canvas top, a dark rear window, a spare wheel mounted flat on the back, and a round tail lamp glowing faintly. The area of gravel immediately in front of the steps is free of tyre marks; tyre tracks only begin some distance down the drive, from where the car had stood parked, and run from that point onward to the gates. Numerous footprints, however, criss-cross the snow thickly in every direction across the full length of the drive, including the ground right in front of the steps - overlapping trails of comings and goings from the household and the investigation, dense enough to blur into a general trampled confusion in places. A high stone park wall, noticeably tall - well above head height - flanks the open gates on both sides and continues along the boundary into the distance, diminished by perspective but still reading as a substantial barrier rather than a low kerb. Snow-covered parkland spreads either side of the long drive, largely open, with only a few bare winter trees scattered well back toward the tree-line. Beyond the open gates, the drive gives way to a country lane. Snow-covered fields and hedgerows spread either side of the lane, with scattered bare winter trees along the field boundaries. Rising above the distant tree-line, roughly centred toward the horizon, the slender stone steeple of a village church is just visible - a small, simple silhouette pointing into the pale sky, suggesting the nearby village and the journey's true destination.

No human figures are visible; the departure is conveyed by the long empty drive and the small departing car. The only living presence in the frame is Chester, the Colonel's ginger cat, whose implied vantage point is the manor's front steps - themselves out of frame behind the viewer, the house never appearing in the composition. Chester is a small, secondary detail low in the foreground, positioned off to one side rather than centred, seen from behind: a modestly sized rounded orange-and-white silhouette with a few darker tabby lines, his back and haunches to the viewer, not dominating the frame or overlapping the drive itself. He sits statue-still and watchful, a quiet incidental presence rather than the focus of the image, with the long stretch of drive stretching away beyond him, the wall and gates small and distant at its far end, and the departing car smaller still. This framing device - cat's-eye-view from the steps, looking outward - replaces any depiction of the house itself.

Title text is rendered as part of the scene, not a separate overlay: bold blocky pixel-art lettering reading "THE END" is positioned in the upper portion of the image against the sky, in the same dark serif-inspired pixel font with hard, unaliased edges used on the game's splash screen, coloured a deep dried-blood red with a thin black pixel outline for legibility against the pale cloud. No subtitle line beneath it.

Colour palette: deep black for the departing car with a single warm highlight on its tail lamp glass, pale gravel and grey-white snow, dark iron gates (open) and tall stone wall, muted winter parkland, fields and hedgerows beyond, a pale grey stone church steeple against the sky, warm ginger-orange and white for Chester, deep red and pale grey-white for the title text, flat overcast sky fading to a slightly warmer, paler tone near the horizon suggesting the close of the day. Cold, quiet, conclusive winter daylight - a sense of departure and resolution, emphasized by the great length of the empty drive. No human figures.
